# Content Voice

Two registers exist in this site's writing. Pick the one that fits the piece; don't blend them within one file.

## 1. Quiet-personal (journal, and most essays)
Established by the existing journal entries and essays ("A Slow Morning", "On the Quietness of Reading", "The Bookshop that Sells Sneakers", "Reading a Country in Translation").
- Opens on a small, concrete moment or object, not an abstraction.
- Short paragraphs, plain declarative sentences.
- First person, admits uncertainty rather than asserting conclusions.
- Ends on a small grounded image, not a stated lesson.
- Journal entries are always this register — 300–500 words is too short for the critical register below.

## 2. Literary-critical (essays about a specific work, prize, translation, or critical debate — optional register)
Modeled on the voice of *Kritikus Sastera* (azriq.blogspot.com), a Malay-language literary criticism blog studied 2026-08-17. Reference only — do not reuse its actual opinions, claims about specific books, or any verbatim text.
- Opens from a real anecdote, book, exchange, or specific reference — not a generic hook.
- Argument-driven via rhetorical questions that expose a tension rather than demanding an answer (e.g. "What happens to a novel that never reaches classical status?").
- Sentence rhythm alternates short declaratives with long, multi-clause meditative sentences; comfortable running a whole section as one unbroken paragraph when the argument's momentum calls for it.
- Formal-yet-personal: intellectually engaged, but still first person and willing to admit "I don't know if I understood this correctly, but—".
- One controlling metaphor per piece, returned to rather than decorated with — not a metaphor per paragraph.
- Closes on a resonant image (often involving someone other than the narrator) that embodies the argument without restating it as a conclusion.
- Minimal humor; tone leans elegiac when the subject is loss, obscurity, or asymmetry (translation, canon, forgotten works).
- Foreign titles/names/quotes can stay untranslated when discussing international work.

## BM adaptations
Adapt the rhetorical-question rhythm and sentence pacing naturally into Malay phrasing — don't translate the English draft literally, and don't translate azriq.blogspot.com's Malay literally into English either.
