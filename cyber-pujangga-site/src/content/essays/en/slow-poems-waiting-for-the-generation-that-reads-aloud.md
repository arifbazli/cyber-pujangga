I first noticed it on an ordinary night.

The afternoon had ended late. My daughter was not yet asleep.
I, for my part, opened my phone — not with any purpose, only
because the body did not want to move anymore. The screen
lit up. I scrolled.

And somewhere between the cat videos and the man imitating a
cat's voice, a sound stopped me. A woman's voice. Slow.
Reading something whose rhythm I had known since childhood.
Not quite a poem. Like someone reading from a notebook, with
dim light.

I stopped. Not because of a great performance. Not because of
a complex text. I stopped because the reading was still
happening. In the middle of everything that is fast, someone
was still reading out loud.

For years I had believed that reading was a slowly shrinking
practice. Unpopular. Not viral. A habit that people still
performed in the corner of a room, like collecting stamps or
folding paper. I saw it as something diminishing, not as
something waiting.

That night I began to think otherwise.

Reading, perhaps, is not shrinking. Perhaps it is only
changing shape. The generation that learns to read on TikTok
does not read the way my generation read in school libraries.
They read on screens, with short pauses, with an ear for
background sound. But they read.

I see it in my daughter's friends. Children who know who
writes popular verse, even though they never learned that
name in class. Children who send each other cuttings of poems,
the way I sent song lyrics in secondary school. The form is
different. The habit is the same.

There may be a generation that is learning to read again, not
from thick books but from thirty-second sounds. They may not
become devoted readers in the classical sense. But they will
become readers who know that text can pause — that there is a
moment when the screen lights up and something slower than
the algorithm takes place there.

I closed the phone that night, not with sadness. I closed it
with patience. Waiting, perhaps. The way one waits for
something that does not need to be forced.

I think I can wait a long time.
